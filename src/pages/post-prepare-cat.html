How to prepare your cat for grooming (without the drama)

Susan, Pawgo Mobile Grooming October 1, 2026 6 min read

Cats don't object to being groomed nearly as often as we assume — they object to where it happens. A strange room, barking dogs, a car ride and a carrier that only ever means vet visits: by the time the brush appears, the cat has already decided this is a hostage situation.

Why grooming at home changes the equation

When the grooming setup comes to your address, three of the biggest escalation steps disappear: the car ride, the waiting room and the other animals. Your cat stays in familiar territory, and the appointment is one-on-one — no dogs barking through a wall, no echoing salon floor.

What's left is the part cats actually dislike: being handled. That part gets easier with preparation, and most of the preparation happens before the groomer ever arrives.

The day before

Do a quick coat check

Run your hands over your cat's coat the day before the appointment. You're feeling for three things: mats (usually behind the ears, under the armpits and around the hindquarters), skin irritation, and anything stuck in the fur.

Note anything you find so it can be mentioned when you book — a mat behind the ear and a general "she's a bit tangled" lead to very different appointments.

Never cut a mat out yourself

It cannot be said enough: cat skin is loose, and mats sit closer to it than they look. Scissors slip, skin pulls into the mat, and a routine groom becomes an emergency vet visit. Leave mats for professional removal.

Write down health notes

Recent procedures, dental work, arthritis, kidney issues, medications, skin conditions, past reactions to handling — all of it matters. Senior cats especially: a shorter, gentler session is usually the right call, and knowing in advance makes that possible.

On the day

Keep your cat indoors

The most common scheduling problem with cats is simple: nobody can find them. Keep cats inside on grooming day so the appointment starts on time instead of becoming a garden search party.

Pick a familiar room, not a hiding place

Cats that are shoved into a laundry cupboard to "wait quietly" arrive at the appointment already escalated — they spent twenty minutes panicking in the dark. A normal, familiar room with you nearby is calmer than any hiding spot.

Keep the morning normal

No big play-fights right before the appointment, no new visitors, no rearranged furniture. Cats take their cues from routine; the more ordinary the morning, the more ordinary the groom feels.

Stay bored yourself

Same rule as with dogs: if you're hovering, apologising and tensing up, your cat reads it. Quiet and unbothered is contagious.

Long-haired vs short-haired prep

Short-haired cats need far less home maintenance — a weekly pass with a soft brush, more during seasonal coat blows. Their grooming sessions are shorter (1.5 hours at Pawgo) because there's less coat to work through.

Long-haired cats should be combed several times a week, with deliberate attention to the friction zones. Mats form where coat rubs against coat or against the ground. Their appointments run longer (2 hours), and if the coat has already gone past saving, a lion cut resets it — body coat taken short, head, mane, tail tip and booties left long.

If you're unsure which your cat needs, check the cat grooming options — sessions are priced by coat length, and the appointment details are confirmed with you when you book.

What to have ready

  • Your cat indoors and findable
  • A familiar room with somewhere for your cat to sit that isn't a sealed cupboard
  • Notes on medication, health conditions and past grooming reactions
  • Anything you found during the coat check
  • Treats for afterwards — post-groom reassurance builds good memories

Worst case first? If your cat is badly matted, book a lion cut (CA$190, about 2 hours) rather than a standard groom — trying to comb out a heavily matted coat in one appointment isn't comfortable for anyone.

After the appointment

Give your cat a quiet, warm place to land. Some cats groom themselves obsessively after a bath; others sleep for hours. Fresh water out, food as normal, and leave them alone for a while — the post-groom hours are not the time for visitors.

Within a few days, resume combing. The coat is cleanest and most detangled right after a professional groom, which makes it the perfect moment to establish the weekly rhythm that keeps the next appointment short. Ten minutes with a comb beats an hour of mat removal later.
