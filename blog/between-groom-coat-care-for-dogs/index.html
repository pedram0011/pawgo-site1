Coat care

Between-groom coat care: keeping your dog's coat healthy at home

Susan, Pawgo Mobile Grooming October 7, 2026 7 min read

A professional groom is a reset button, not a force field. Between appointments, coats do what coats do: they shed, tangle, trap dirt and mat at friction points. Ten minutes a week at home is what keeps the next groom easy — and cheap.

Match the tool to the coat

Using the wrong brush is why most home brushing routines die in week two. If the tool doesn't suit the coat, it either does nothing or hurts — and your dog learns that brushing equals discomfort.

Slicker brush

Fine, short wire bristles on a flat or curved pad. The workhorse for curly and non-shedding coats (Poodles, Doodles, Bichons) and for finishing long, silky coats. Use light pressure — you're untangling, not scrubbing.

Steel comb

The most underrated tool in the box. After any brushing session, run a greyhound comb through the coat. If it glides, you're done. If it catches, you've found the spot the brush missed — which is exactly where mats form.

Undercoat rake & deshedding tool

For double coats (Huskies, Goldens, Labs, Pomeranians, Shepherds). These reach the loose undercoat beneath the topcoat without cutting either layer. This is where most of the hair on your sofa actually lives.

Bristle brush

For smooth, short coats (Boxers, Beagles, Bulldogs). It won't dematt anything, but it distributes skin oils and takes off loose surface hair — five minutes, big difference in shine.

The five zones where mats start

Every brushing session should hit these spots first, because they're where friction lives:

  • Behind the ears — constant contact with the head and collar
  • Under the armpits — where legs rub against the body with every step
  • The groin and inner thighs — warm, damp, high-friction
  • Behind the hind legs — drags against the ground and the other leg
  • The collar line — where the collar traps hair against the skin

These five spots take about two minutes to check properly, and they're where mat-removal appointments almost always end up focusing.

A routine that survives real life

Frequency depends on coat, but a routine beats an ambitious schedule you abandon. Pick a fixed moment — Sunday evening, Saturday morning — and attach brushing to it.

  • Curly / non-shedding coats: comb through 3–4 times a week
  • Long, silky coats: brush 2–3 times a week plus a comb check
  • Double coats: rake weekly, daily during the two annual coat blows
  • Smooth coats: a bristle brush pass once a week

Always work in sections and finish with the comb. If the comb stops, the brushing wasn't finished — it just looked finished.

Bathing at home: less is more

Most dogs don't need weekly baths. Over-bathing strips natural oils, and a dog with dry skin and a dull coat is usually a dog that's being washed too often. Between professional grooms, a rinse after a muddy walk is often all that's needed.

If you do bathe at home: brush thoroughly before the bath (wet mats are tighter mats), use a dog-appropriate shampoo, rinse twice as long as you think necessary, and dry completely — a damp coat left to air-dry in a thick coat is exactly how hotspots and tangles start.

Nails, ears and teeth are part of coat care

They get their own line because they're the three things owners skip. Long nails change how a dog stands and walks; dirty ears become vet visits; plaque builds quietly. Nail trims are included in full groom and deshedding packages at Pawgo, and standalone nail appointments (15 minutes, CA$42) cover the gap between grooms.

When home care isn't enough

Some situations genuinely need a professional rather than patience:

  • Mats that have tightened against the skin. Stop brushing and book mat removal — dragging a brush through tight matting just pulls the skin.
  • A coat blow in progress. Home tools manage it; a deshedding appointment clears it.
  • Coat that's outgrown its shape. No amount of brushing replaces a haircut.
  • A dog who's decided brushing is unpleasant. Short, positive sessions and professional help beat daily arguments.

Never cut mats out with scissors. Both dog and cat skin folds up into mats, and scissors find the fold before you see it. Mat removal at Pawgo starts at CA$120 and is done safely with the right tools.

Put the next appointment in the diary

The best coat-care habit is the boring one: book the next groom before you leave the current one. An appointment held at the right interval means the coat never has the chance to become an emergency — see how often to groom your dog for the intervals by coat type.
