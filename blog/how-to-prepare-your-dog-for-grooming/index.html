Preparation

How to prepare your dog for a grooming appointment

Susan, Pawgo Mobile Grooming September 24, 2026 6 min read

Most grooming-day stress is created the night before. Five minutes of prep — a brush through, a note written down, a space cleared — turns a tense appointment into a boring one. Boring is the goal.

The day before

1. Brush out tangles while the coat is dry

This is the single most useful thing you can do. Mats tighten when they get wet, and a bath turns a small tangle into a knot you can't fit a comb through. Spend ten minutes with a brush or comb the day before, working from the skin outwards rather than dragging from the tips.

Pay attention to the friction zones: behind the ears, under the armpits, around the groin and behind the hind legs. That's where mats start on almost every long-coated dog.

2. Don't attempt a DIY trim

It's tempting to even things up with kitchen scissors before the appointment. Please don't. Skin folds into mats — a mat that looks flat against the body often sits a few millimetres above a skin fold, and scissors find that fold first. Leave cutting to the groomer and bring the coat in as it is.

3. Write down anything medical

Recent surgeries or procedures, skin conditions, allergies, medications, lumps, ear infections, sore hips, needle-phobia, sound sensitivity, past negative grooming experiences — all of it changes how a groom should be handled. A quick note in your phone, then mention it when you book, is enough.

4. Make some space

If your groomer is mobile, they need somewhere to park and a bit of room to set up near your door. Move the bins, free the driveway, and put away anything the dog could knock over in excitement.

On the day

Exercise first

A walk or a game of fetch before the appointment burns off the burst of energy that otherwise arrives as bouncing, spinning or barking during the groom. Tired dogs are cooperative dogs.

Watch the feeding schedule

A full stomach plus a bath plus being stood up isn't comfortable. Feed a normal meal earlier in the day rather than right before the appointment time.

Keep your dog findable

Nothing derails a start time like chasing a dog around the yard. Keep dogs indoors or in the yard on grooming day, with a collar or harness ready for the handover.

Stay calm yourself

Dogs read their owners. If you're rushing, apologising and hovering, your dog decides something is wrong. A normal morning — and a simple "this is Susan, she's here for your groom" — sets a far better tone.

What to have ready

  • The brush or comb you normally use, if you have one
  • Treats, for afterwards especially
  • Your notes on health, medication and behaviour
  • Collar or harness on your dog for the handover
  • Any questions about the finish you want — ask before the groom starts, not after

Nervous dog? Mention it when you book. The mobile setup already removes the three biggest stressors — the car ride, the cage and the other dogs — and knowing in advance lets the appointment be paced around your dog.

After the groom

Your dog will probably sleep for hours — that's normal after a bath, a dryer and two hours of being a good sport. Offer water, give them a quiet spot, and do a quick hands-on check the next day: run your palms over the coat for small tangles and look at the skin for any redness or nicks.

Then start the next appointment on the calendar. Coats are much easier to maintain than to catch up on — see how often to groom your dog for the intervals by coat type, and the between-groom care guide for the weekly routine that keeps coats easy.

Your five-minute checklist

  1. Brush the coat the day before — dry, from the skin out
  2. No DIY scissor work
  3. Note anything medical or behavioural
  4. Clear parking and door space
  5. Exercise, then feed earlier than usual
  6. Keep your dog findable and ready
  7. Ask questions before the groom starts
