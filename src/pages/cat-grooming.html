Coat care

Short hair, long hair & lion cuts

The right session depends on what your cat's coat is actually doing.

Short-haired cats

Domestic Shorthairs look low-maintenance, and mostly they are — until loose winter coat, dander or a missed patch of matting behind the ears says otherwise. A short-hair session is the lightest option at 1.5 hours.

Long-haired cats

Persians, Ragdolls, Maine Coons and long-haired mixes mat more easily, especially around the belly, armpits and behind the hind legs. Long-haired sessions run 2 hours to allow for the extra combing and drying.

Lion cuts

A lion cut takes the body coat down short while leaving the head, mane, tail tip and booties — the classic lion silhouette. It's the practical choice for badly matted or heat-burdened long coats, and it takes about 2 hours.

Grown-out coats can be reset with a lion cut and maintained with regular long-hair sessions afterwards. If you're unsure which your cat needs, book the closest match — the session is confirmed with you when you book.

Preparing your cat

Help your cat arrive calm

Cats read your energy — a relaxed owner makes for a relaxed cat.

  • Keep your cat indoors on grooming day so they're easy to find when Susan arrives.
  • Leave your cat in a familiar room rather than hiding them somewhere stressful.
  • Share anything Susan should know — health conditions, sensitivities, past grooming reactions.
  • A calm morning helps — skip the energetic play-fight right before the appointment.

Aftercare

Keeping the coat good for longer

Ten minutes a week at home stretches the gap between professional sessions.

  • Comb long-haired cats a few times a week, always working from the skin outwards.
  • Check behind the ears, under the arms and the belly — that's where mats start.
  • Never cut a mat out with scissors — skin folds into mats and cuts easily.
